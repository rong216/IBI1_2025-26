Reflection
This approach helped me understand how to implement infectious disease models in Python in the form of probabilistic models. The first SIR model is the easiest part to understand because its logic is clear: susceptible populations can be infected, and infected individuals can recover. The spatial SIR model is the most challenging part for me because I have to carefully consider how to locate infected cells, detect their neighboring areas, avoid exceeding the grid range, and update population data correctly. The most important conclusion I have drawn from this practice is that disease modeling not only involves equation derivation, but also requires translating biological concepts into programmable code. In addition, the random model produces slightly different results each time it runs, reflecting the randomness of real-world processes.
